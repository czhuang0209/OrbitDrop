Three steps.
That's the whole manual.

01

Drag a file. Tap Shift.

Start dragging anything in File Explorer or on your desktop, and keep holding. Tap Shift once and the wheel opens right under your pointer.

02

Let go on what you want.

Drop it on JPG, PNG, PDF or anything else. A new file lands next to the original, which is never changed. Changed your mind? Let go in the middle, or press Esc.

03

Tap again for more.

Each tap of Shift switches the wheel: Convert for formats, Tools for quick edits like compress and resize, and Actions for copy, move, share and zip.

Small wheel.
Surprisingly deep.

Options open into more.

Slices with dots, like Compress, open their choices when you rest on them: 50%, under 1 MB, under 200 KB. Let go straight away for the most common one.

No dragging needed.

Select files and press Ctrl Alt Space. The wheel opens where you are; click an option. Click the centre to switch to Tools and Actions.

Make the wheel yours.

In Settings, your wheel is right there. Click a slice to hide it, drag slices into your own order, press Save. Done.

A photo editor, built in.

Crop, adjust brightness, contrast and warmth, draw arrows and boxes, add text, or redact. It always saves a copy.

Everything it does.

Convert

  • Images to JPG, PNG, GIF, BMP, TIFF or PDF
  • Opens WebP, HEIC, AVIF, JPEG XL and camera RAW*
  • Several images into one PDF
  • PDF pages to PNG or JPG
  • Video to MP4 or WMV
  • Audio to MP3, M4A, WAV, WMA or FLAC
  • The soundtrack out of a video

Tools

  • Photo editor: crop, adjust, mark up, redact
  • Compress photos to a size, like under 1 MB
  • Resize, rotate, flip, grayscale
  • Copy the text out of an image
  • Trim and join videos, make GIFs, snapshots
  • Normalize audio, mono or stereo
  • See and edit details, remove location or all metadata

Actions

  • Copy to or move to a folder
  • Share with Nearby Share and apps
  • Zip, or extract ZIP and TAR
  • Float files on an always-on-top shelf
  • Open a terminal here
  • Copy the path or name
  • Set a photo as wallpaper

*When Windows has the matching image extension installed.

Questions.

Is it really free?

Yes. No trial, no ads, no account. It's open source, too.

Which Windows versions does it work on?

Windows 11, and Windows 10 version 2004 or later, 64-bit.

Does it change my original files?

Never. Every result is a new file saved next to the original, with a new name if one already exists.

Does it need the internet?

No. Everything runs on your PC. The Microsoft Store handles updates.

Can I use it without dragging?

Yes. Select files in File Explorer and press Ctrl Alt Space.

Where does it live when I'm not using it?

In the notification area, next to the clock. Click the icon for Settings and the guide. You can have it start when you sign in.

Something isn't working. Where do I report it?

Open an issue on GitHub. Settings → Advanced → Logs has details that help.
